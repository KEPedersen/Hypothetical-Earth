How it works

The globe is rotated so that 21.5°N, 89.5°E moves to the top: a turn of 89.5° about today's axis, then a tilt of 68.5°. A rotation keeps every coastline and distance unchanged; only the grid of latitude and longitude moves. Each place's new latitude is 90° minus its great-circle distance from the new pole. The Arctic Circle is drawn at today's 66°34′, which assumes Earth's axial tilt stays the same.
